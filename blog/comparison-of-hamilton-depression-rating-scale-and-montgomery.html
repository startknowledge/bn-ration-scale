Introduction to Hamilton Depression Rating Scale and Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale

The Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAM-D) is a widely used rating scale for assessing the severity of depression in patients. Developed by Max Hamilton in 1960, the HAM-D is a 17-item or 21-item scale that evaluates the presence and severity of depressive symptoms, including mood, guilt, suicide, insomnia, and weight loss (Hamilton, 1960). The Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS), on the other hand, is a 10-item scale developed by Montgomery and Åsberg in 1979 (Montgomery & Åsberg, 1979). The MADRS is designed to assess the severity of depressive symptoms, including apparent sadness, reported sadness, inner tension, reduced sleep, reduced appetite, concentration difficulties, lassitude, inability to feel, and pessimistic thoughts.

A comparison of the HAM-D and MADRS reveals both similarities and differences between the two scales. Both scales are widely used in clinical practice and research to assess the severity of depression. The MADRS, on the other hand, focuses more on the psychological and emotional symptoms of depression, such as apparent sadness and inner tension.
